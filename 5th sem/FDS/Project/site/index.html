The calculator runs a compact gradient-boosting model exported from Python — 150 trees, 0.903 ROC-AUC on the held-out test set, trained on the same cleaned data and split as the project notebook. The report’s final model is a tuned 200-iteration model on 20 inputs and scores 0.898; the small difference is expected. Fields not shown here (order value, weight, country, segment, package size, payment method, carrier) are held at the dataset’s typical value, so the score describes an otherwise typical order with the conditions you set.

How it works

From order conditions to a probability

The same pipeline as the report, minus the pieces a browser cannot run: median imputation, scaling, one-hot encoding and then the ensemble of trees.

1

Encode the order

Numeric fields are standardised with the training set’s means and scales; categorical fields are one-hot encoded. Three engineered features are derived: weather severity, priority rank and distance per promised day.

2

150 trees vote

Each small tree splits on thresholds learned from 40,000 orders. The trees are fitted sequentially, each correcting the error left by the ones before it, and their sum is passed through a logistic function.

3

Report the risk

The result is a probability between 0 and 1. The band is a reading aid, not a business rule: 0.45 and 0.65 are thresholds for display, chosen around the 56.3% base rate.

No leakage. In the project every transform is fitted inside the pipeline, per fold, so a validation row never contributes to a median or a category list. The exported demo freezes exactly those training statistics. The test set was used once, at the end.

Why boosting wins: non-linear splits capture the weather × distance interaction, and sequential fitting concentrates later trees on the storm / long-haul cells earlier ones miss. Logistic regression trails because its boundary is linear — its train and CV scores are identical, so it underfits rather than overfits.

What the data showed

Risk is steep, and it compounds

Late rate in snow versus clear weather — a 58-point spread. Storm is 91.0%.
